# KiCad project for the fan controller: schematic and a fab-ready PCB

Date: 2026-11-22. Status: awaiting approval; all questions settled.

## Intent

A KiCad 10 project in the repository that holds the controller's schematic and a two-layer printed circuit board, complete enough to upload to a PCB fab (JLCPCB, PCBWay, Aisler or any shop with ordinary two-layer rules) and populate from the parts already in use. The circuit is the one running on the perfboard today, plus two protections the perfboard never had: a resettable fuse on the 12 V input and a reverse-polarity diode. The firmware needs no change.

Why: the perfboard build is a one-off that takes an afternoon and carries the hazards `docs/board/` warns about (bare 12 V next to flash pins, insulated leads over stubs). A fab board makes the next copy a matter of soldering parts into labelled holes, and gives the project a proper schematic.

## Goals

- Schematic that passes ERC with no errors and reads cleanly as a printed page.
- PCB that passes DRC with no errors and no unconnected items, fully routed, with Gerbers, drill files and a BOM exported and committed, so anyone can order it without running anything.
- Netlist derived from `docs/board/data.py` (plus the two new parts), so schematic, PCB and perfboard plan agree by construction.
- Everything generated by a script in the repo and checked by `kicad-cli`, the way `docs/board/gen.py` produces the perfboard plan; the generated KiCad files are committed too.
- Same mounting as the perfboard (70 × 50 mm, four M3 corner holes) and connectors on the same edges, so it drops into the same place.

## Non-goals

- Changing the circuit beyond the fuse and the diode. No bulk input capacitor, USB-serial chip, tach filters, test points, or SMD parts.
- An enclosure, 3D models beyond what the stock libraries provide, or a fab-specific assembly order (no pick-and-place; this is a hand-soldered through-hole board).
- Replacing the perfboard plan. `docs/board/` stays as it is.
- A schematic drawn by hand in the GUI. The script is the source; the files are its output.

## Anti-goals (would make it a failure even if it "works")

- A board that passes DRC but cannot be built or used: antenna over copper, a connector whose plug does not fit at the edge, a TO-220 that collides with a header, pin 1 of a sensor header unmarked.
- Rules at the fab's minimum. Tracks and clearances stay well inside every basic fab's limits, so the first order is not a gamble.
- A generated schematic that is a tangle of wires. It must be readable by someone who has never seen the project.
- Any divergence between the schematic's nets, the PCB's nets, and `data.py`.

## Constraints

- KiCad 10.0.6 as installed: `kicad-cli` (ERC, DRC, exports), the `pcbnew` Python module, the stock symbol and footprint libraries. No extra packages.
- The schematic file has no scripting API, so it is written directly as KiCad s-expressions. The board is built through `pcbnew`.
- Through-hole parts, the same ones as the perfboard: ESP-12F, Mini-360 buck module, 2N3904 (×3), IRF9540N, 1N5819, 10 kΩ/4.7 kΩ/1 kΩ axial resistors, 100 nF 2.54 mm ceramic, 6 × 6 mm tact switches, 2.54 mm pin headers, 4-pin PC fan headers (Molex KK 254 / 47053 style), 5.08 mm screw terminal.
- The ESP-12F's antenna must not sit over copper. Its boot-pin rules (GPIO0, 2, 15, EN, RST pull-ups and pull-down) are already in the netlist.
- 12 V paths carry up to about 1.5 A normally (three fans, the buck, the flow meter) and the fuse's trip current briefly.
- The sensors' headers and the flow header can be plugged reversed, so pin 1 is marked on silkscreen and labelled.

## Approaches considered

1. **Generator script in the repo (chosen).** Python writes the schematic s-expressions (stock symbols copied into the file, positions and wires from a layout table) and builds the board via `pcbnew` (stock footprints plus a custom Mini-360 footprint, placement table, tracks from waypoint lists, ground pour). `kicad-cli` runs ERC and DRC and exports PDF, SVG, Gerbers, drill, BOM and a 3D render. Reproducible, reviewable as text, no dependencies, consistent with how the repo already does the board plan. Cost: the routing is specified by hand and iterated against DRC.
2. **Outside libraries: SKiDL or kiutils for the files, Freerouting for routing.** Fewer lines to write, but new packages (Freerouting needs Java and is not in the distribution's repos), autorouted boards need cleanup, and kiutils lags behind KiCad 10's file format. Rejected.
3. **Schematic and placement by script, routing by the user in KiCad.** Leaves the fab-ready part undone and the routing is lost on the next regeneration. Rejected.

## The design, as decided

### Circuit

The perfboard circuit, from `docs/board/data.py`, with these changes:

- **F1, resettable polyfuse**, in series with the +12 V input: J2's + terminal → F1 → the protected `+12V` net that feeds everything else (Q3's source, the Mini-360, the flow header, D2). Hold current 2.5 A, trip 5 A, radial 5.1 mm pitch (Bourns MF-R250 or Littelfuse RXEF250; any ≥ 16 V radial polyfuse of that pitch fits). The rating allows for a PTC's hold current falling about 15 % at 40 °C next to a radiator, and three fans of up to 0.5 A each plus the buck; a nuisance trip would cut the board's own power, the one failure the firmware cannot cover. The footprint's courtyard takes the larger discs of the series (up to MF-R300), so the value can change without a board change.
- **D2, reverse-polarity diode across the input**, cathode to `+12V`, anode to GND, behind the fuse. A reversed supply flows through D2 and trips F1; nothing downstream sees it. Normal operation loses no voltage. A 3 A rectifier with a high surge rating, 1N5401 (DO-201AD), horizontal.
- Net names: `+12V_IN` (J2 to F1), `+12V` (fused, unswitched), `+12V_SW` (behind Q3), `3V3`, `GND`, and the perfboard's signal names (`PWM`, `TACH1..3`, `DQ`, `FLOW`, the IOn pins, `RST`, `EN`, `TX`, `RX`, `GATE`, `Q1_B`, `Q2_B`, `Q4_B`).

Everything else is unchanged: the ESP-12F with R1/R2/R3/R9/R10/R11 pull-ups and pull-down and C1, SW1 RST and SW2 FLASH to GND, J1 serial, the Mini-360 12 V → 3.3 V, Q1 PWM driver with R5, Q2/R6/R12/Q3 12 V switch with D1 across the switched rail, J3–J5 fan headers (GND, +12V_SW, TACHn, PWM) with R7/R8/R4 pull-ups, J7 flow header on unswitched +12V with R13/Q4/R14, J6/J8 DS18B20 headers on 3V3/DQ/GND.

### Board

- **Outline 70 × 50 mm**, 1.6 mm, two copper layers. Four M3 mounting holes (3.2 mm) 4 mm from each corner's edges, plated and connected to GND with a 6 mm pad, so a metal case screw can ground the board and a nylon screw changes nothing. The pattern is new (decided by the user): nothing has to match the diymore perfboard's holes.
- **Edges**, as on the perfboard: 12 V screw terminal and flow header on the left edge, three fan headers along the bottom edge with their latches toward the edge so the plugs clear the board, sensor headers and serial header on the right edge. Buttons near the left edge where a finger reaches them. Nothing but the module on the top edge.
- **ESP-12F at the top edge**, no copper or parts within 3 mm of the module's sides. Its antenna section overhangs the top edge (decided by the user: there is room past the edge where the board lives), so no copper keep-out is spent inside the outline. The module is soldered on its castellated pads; the six flash pads are on the footprint but unconnected.
- **Q3 (TO-220) upright**, tab toward the fan headers, with 3 mm of clear board behind it. Nothing tall within reach of the tab.
- **Copper**: GND pour on the back, stitched to a front GND where traces need it. Tracks: 2 mm for the 12 V trunk (`+12V_IN`, `+12V` to Q3, `+12V_SW` from Q3 to the fan header row, the terminal's GND return); 1.5 mm for each fan header's `+12V_SW` and GND spur and the Mini-360's and flow header's 12 V; 0.5 mm for `3V3`; 0.3 mm for signals. The 2 mm trunk survives the fuse's trip current and a shorted fan's surge long enough for the fuse or the supply's own protection to act. Clearance 0.25 mm everywhere, vias 0.8 mm with a 0.4 mm drill. Those are two to three times the minimum of every basic two-layer service.
- **Silkscreen**: reference and value for every part, pin 1 marked and every pin named at the headers (`GND +12V TACH PWM`; `+12V GND SIG`; `3V3 DQ GND`; `TX RX GND`; `+ −`), `RST` and `FLASH` at the buttons, the TO-220's orientation, the project name, revision and the repository URL. The diode's and polyfuse's orientation marks.
- **Rev 1.0** in the title block of both files.

### Schematic

One A4 landscape sheet, drawn in functional blocks left to right and top to bottom: power input (J2, F1, D2, Mini-360, C1), the module with its pull-ups and buttons and serial header, the PWM driver, the 12 V switch with D1, the three fan headers, the flow stage, the two sensor headers. GPIO nets travel by net label, not by long wires. Power symbols for `+12V`, `+12V_SW`, `3V3`, `GND`; a PWR_FLAG where ERC needs one; no-connect flags on the module's unused pins (ADC, the flash pads). Footprints assigned in the schematic, so the PCB's netlist is the schematic's.

### Files and tooling

- Directory `hardware/kicad/`: `gen.py` (the generator; `python3 gen.py` writes everything and runs the checks), `layout.py` (schematic positions, PCB placement, routes, as data), the project files `fan_controller.kicad_pro`, `fan_controller.kicad_sch`, `fan_controller.kicad_pcb`, a project library `fan_controller.kicad_sym` with the Mini-360 symbol and `fan_controller.pretty/` with its footprint, `sym-lib-table`/`fp-lib-table` for them, and `out/` with `schematic.pdf`, `gerbers.zip` (Gerbers + drill + job file), `bom.csv`, `board-top.png`, `board-bottom.png`, `erc.txt`, `drc.txt`.
- The netlist comes from `docs/board/data.py` imported by path; `layout.py` adds F1 and D2 and renames the three 12 V nets. `gen.py` fails if `data.py`'s pin → net map and the schematic's disagree, and if ERC or DRC reports an error.
- README gets a short "Ordering the PCB" section (what to upload, what to buy, the fuse and diode); CLAUDE.md gets a paragraph on `hardware/kicad/` like the one on `docs/board/`; the research doc's parts list gains the fuse and the diode.

## Left to the builder

- Exact placement coordinates and route paths, the symbol positions and wire paths on the schematic, and whether routes are hand-listed waypoints or come from a small grid router inside `gen.py` (either is fine as long as DRC is clean and the design rules above hold).
- Which stock footprint variants (resistor lead pitch, diode pitch, header variants). The polyfuse footprint is drawn by hand (the stock library has no radial MF-R), 5.1 mm pitch with a courtyard for the series' discs.
- The Mini-360 footprint's drawing: four pads at the module's real spacing, 15.24 mm along by 9.144 mm across (`data.py`'s U2 note; the perfboard's 7.62 mm was the grid's compromise and its bent stubs do not carry over), oval pads with a 1.2 mm drill so clones that vary by ±0.1 mm still take straight pin headers; the module stands on short pin headers, trimmer up and reachable, and its courtyard is the module's 17 × 11 mm plus the headers' height clearance from Q3 and the module keep-out.
- The structure of `gen.py`, how the s-expressions are emitted, and how the KiCad file-format version is pinned (KiCad 10 writes `2026xxxx` versions; `kicad-cli sch upgrade`/`pcb upgrade` exist).
- Silkscreen typography and the exact wording of labels.
- Whether `out/` is regenerated by a one-line script or by `gen.py` itself.

## Verification

- `kicad-cli sch erc`: 0 errors. `kicad-cli pcb drc`: 0 errors, 0 unconnected, 0 schematic-parity issues.
- Nets: the pin → net map read back from the generated schematic equals `data.py`'s plus the additions; the PCB's nets equal the schematic's (`kicad-cli sch export netlist` compared with the board).
- Visual: `kicad-cli` SVG/PDF of the schematic and `kicad-cli pcb render` of both sides, reviewed for the anti-goals above (antenna clear, plugs clear the edge, Q3 clear, labels present).
- Gerbers re-opened: `kicad-cli pcb export gerbers` output plus the drill file imported back into a scratch board (via `pcbnew`'s gerber import is not available; opening in `gerbview` is a manual step for the user) — at minimum the job file lists every layer and the drill file is non-empty.
- The project opens in KiCad 10 without conversion warnings (the user checks this once).
